# Community briefing sheet

For the people who live in the hexagon. Not lawyers, not reporters.

Plain language. Short sentences. No statutory jargon where an ordinary word
works, and where a legal term is unavoidable, say what it means. The reader
wants to know what the data says about where they live and what they can do.

`what_this_means` must be honest about the limits. The score describes modelled
exposure, nearby permitted sources and a vulnerable population. It is not a
finding of wrongdoing by any operator, it is not a health diagnosis, it does not
say anybody's illness was caused by anything, and a Louisiana percentile is a
Louisiana percentile and not a national one.

`what_you_can_do` lists concrete steps actually available: commenting on a
pending permit, attending a hearing, requesting records, contacting a named
agency. Never tell the reader they have a legal claim, that they should sue,
that they are likely to win anything, or what they personally should do about
their own health or property. Those are the two things this document must never
become: legal advice, and medical advice.

If a Title VI complaint is mentioned, it is a complaint filed with EPA, not a
lawsuit.
