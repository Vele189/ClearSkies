Nearly every site improved, and coverage improved with it: AirToxScreen models
every tract, so hexes that previously had no Exposures value acquired one.
Geismar moved from unevaluable to measurable, which is progress even though it
now records a failure — §13 counts `not_applicable` as not-a-pass either way.

The pass was lost on Chalmette alone, which fell 13.9 points. Percentiles are
relative: adding a statewide modelled surface lifted other places more than it
lifted Chalmette. That is worth understanding before anything is concluded from
it, and it is not evidence that E1 and E2 should be removed.

## The negative controls are the more serious failure

| Control | 13 indicators | 15 indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Old Metairie | fail, 75.5 | fail, 86.9 |
| Bocage | fail, 94.2 | fail, 95.2 |
| South Lafayette | pass, 37.1 | pass, 41.3 |
| Mandeville | not applicable | not applicable |

A control passes only if the score declines to flag any part of it. Two affluent
neighbourhoods rank in the top decile or near it, and both rose when the
pollution data improved.

The component breakdown for Bocage's anchor cell shows where this comes from:
Population Characteristics is correctly low — `sensitive` 33.8 and
`socioeconomic` 38.8 — while `env_effects` is 86.1, because Baton Rouge has
facilities within the 10 km interaction radius of every neighbourhood in it,
affluent or not. The population half is working. The pollution half is not
distinguishing between living near industry and being burdened by it.

§13.5's alternative-specification check rules out the obvious remedy: moving
Environmental Effects from 0.5 to 1.0 leaves a 0.982 rank correlation and
dropping the group entirely leaves 0.957, so reweighting that group would change
almost nothing. The leave-one-out check points instead at Sensitive Populations,
where S1 or S2 alone can move a quarter of the state. Both are §8 questions for
the methodology paper.
